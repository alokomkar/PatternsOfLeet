Our story

A palace recipe, kept exactly as it was given

The lineage

Made by the descendants of Kakasura Madappa

Guru Sweet Mart is run by the descendants of Kakasura Madappa, the Mysore Palace cook traditionally credited with creating Mysore Pak. The recipe has been kept within the same family since — not reformulated for scale, not adapted for a wider palate. The proportions of gram flour, sugar and ghee, and the slow, hands-on cooking method, have stayed the same.

Mysuru · Palace Kitchen Lineage

How it is made

From gram flour to the cutting table

Step 1

Gram flour preparation

Fine gram flour (besan) is measured and sifted for a smooth, lump-free base.

Step 2

Clarified ghee, poured slowly

Pure ghee is added in stages while the mixture cooks, never rushed.

Step 3

The mixture comes together

Sugar syrup, gram flour and ghee are cooked together until the texture is exactly right — a matter of feel, learned over years.

Step 4

Poured into the tray

The hot mixture is poured into trays and left to set just long enough.

Step 5

Hand-cut, while still warm

Every piece is hand-cut — the texture and porosity that make Mysore Pak recognisable come from this step.

Two shops, Mysuru

Still made where it always was

Both Guru Sweet Mart shops are in Mysuru, including the original counter at Devaraja Market. Customers travel from across India, and increasingly order ahead through this website, but the sweets themselves are still made the same way, in the same city.
