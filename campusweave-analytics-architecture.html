ANALYTICS ARCHITECTURE

What's measured, for whom, and where AI actually adds judgment

Every component in the master architecture — client, edge, application, data, and every external provider — has its own operational metrics. Every role — parent, centre head, counsellor, teacher, finance, vendor, platform engineering — has its own business metrics, scoped to what that role is actually allowed to see. Most of this needs zero new tables: it's timestamp aggregation over the 36-table schema already built. Two tables were added specifically because an analytics claim on this page needed them — analytics_events (behavioral events with no domain-table home) and payment_installments (EMI schedule, without which "default rate" isn't computable). Every metric below cites the field it actually reads.

DATA MODEL

Derived vs. captured

Two different sources feed every dashboard on this page.

Derived — no new tableFunnel steps (search → appointment → enquiry → application → payment → admission → enrolment), payment success rates, attendance trends, fulfilment SLA — all computable from timestamps and statuses already in the operational schema (appointments.created_at, payments.status, attendance.date, etc.).
Captured — analytics_eventsBehavioral events with no natural home in a domain table: a search performed (with filters used), a campus profile viewed, a notification opened, an AI proposal reviewed. One flexible table (event_type, actor_type/id, entity_type/id, properties jsonb, occurred_at) instead of a narrow table per event type.

AI-GENERATED INSIGHT

Where AI adds judgment, not just a restated number

Every row below is a proposal written to ai_proposals — reviewed and actioned by a person, never auto-executed. Same rule as the AI-copilot flow throughout this project.

DomainExample generated insightSurfaced toWhat AI does NOT do
Admissions"Enquiries without a counsellor follow-up within 24h convert 40% less often to application — 6 enquiries are approaching that window right now."Counsellor / centre headEnquiry list re-sorted by urgency; no auto-action taken
Payments"Netbanking has an 18% decline rate vs. 2% for UPI this month — consider defaulting checkout to UPI."Finance / school adminSuggestion surfaced on the finance dashboard; checkout default unchanged until a person approves
Academics"Attendance for 4 children in Nursery-A has dropped below 80% over 2 weeks."Teacher / centre headFlagged in the teacher's class view; no parent notification sent automatically
Discovery"Document upload is the top drop-off point in the application flow (22% abandon here) — likely a UX issue, not a policy one."Platform / productSurfaced as a product-review item, not a config change
Commerce"Vendor X is missing its 48h fulfilment SLA (order_items.assigned_at → delivered_at) on 30% of orders this month, vs. 4% for other vendors on the same catalogue."School admin / platformFlagged for vendor conversation; no automatic order reassignment
The pattern that matters more than any single insight: a dashboard tells you a number went down. AI's actual job here is naming why — correlating a metric with a specific upstream cause (channel, staff response time, UX step, vendor) precisely enough that a person can act on it without doing the correlation themselves. If it can't name a specific cause, it shouldn't generate the insight.
